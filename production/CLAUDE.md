# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Projects

- `NoMansLand/`: the original Pygame WWI trench-battle game (one 7,700-line `no_mans_land.py`). It is reference material only; nothing builds from it except `greatwar3d/tools/export_data.py`, which reads its 1914 border polygons with `ast` into `tools/world.poly`.
- `greatwar3d/`: **Great War 1906**, a C++17 + raylib 5.5 3D grand-strategy game (world map, start date 1 Jan 1906), set in an alternative history: `greatwar3d/WORLD.md` says what differs and who rules where, and new content must agree with it. `greatwar3d/README.md` has the controls, feature list and data-format reference.

## Commands (run in `greatwar3d/`)

- `make`: build `./greatwar`. The first build clones raylib 5.5 into `third_party/raylib` and builds `libraylib.a`.
- `make run`: play.
- `make towns`: regenerate `tools/towns_1906.txt` (the smaller towns that divide big provinces) with `tools/gen_towns.py`; run `make map` after it.
- `make map`: regenerate `data/provinces.txt` + `data/provinces.map` with `tools/mapgen` (downloads Natural Earth admin-1 into `tools/_dl` the first time). Province edits go in `tools/cities_1906.txt`, never in the generated `data/provinces.txt`: the line order must match the map file. `make imagery` rebuilds `data/terrain.jpg` (the painted Terrain-mode image, from `tools/_dl/earth.jpg`) and `data/elevation.png` (ImageMagick; from the SRTM ramp on Wikimedia Commons, 0 at sea level and 25.1 m a step). `make geo` rebuilds `data/geo.txt` (rivers, lakes, seas, towns) with `tools/export_geo.py`, which also maps modern town names to 1906 ones.
- `make check` or `./greatwar --simulate DAYS [SEED]`: headless. Loads every data file, prints load warnings, lets the AI play, and prints wars, capitulations, government changes and where the great powers end up. Use this after any sim or data change.
- `GW_SHOT=/path/out.png ./greatwar`: renders a scene, saves a screenshot and exits. It's the way to verify visuals without a human. Extra env vars: `GW_AUTO=TAG` (start as that nation), `GW_DAYS=n` (simulate first), `GW_TAB=1..12` (open a panel, in `enum Tab` order), `GW_PPAGE=0..3` (Production screen page), `GW_CAM=lat,lon,dist`, `GW_MODE=n` (map mode), `GW_HOUR=h` (time of day: day and night), `GW_SEL=Province`, `GW_PTAB=0..2` (province panel page), `GW_COUNTRY=TAG` (country panel), `GW_SELDIVS=1`, `GW_KEEPCHOICE=1` (keep event popups), `GW_PICK=TAG` (nation-pick screen), `GW_MENU=1`, `GW_SETTINGS=1`, `GW_SOLAR=dist` (the Solar System view, 8 to 700), `GW_WAR=TAG` (declare war first), `GW_BUILD=0..3` (Industry build tool), `GW_FTAB=n` / `GW_TTAB=n` (focus and research tab), `GW_FLEET=dist` (select the first fleet and go to it; `GW_FMISSION=n` sets its mission), `GW_ALLTECH=1` (the player knows every tech), `GW_SLOTS=n` (extra research slots of both kinds), `GW_DESCAT=n` / `GW_DESPICK=n` / `GW_TMPLPICK=n` (designer category, open module list (-2 chassis), open template cell list), `GW_CAMPAIGN=n` (start a campaign for that ideology), `GW_DONE=id,id` (report these techs or focuses as finished), `GW_SELARMY=n` (select the player's nth army), `GW_PLAN=front:TAG` / `defend:Prov;Prov` / `attack:Prov;Prov` (give the selected army a plan; `GW_EXEC=1` starts the attack), `GW_CMDPICK=army id` (open its general list), `GW_FSAIL=Prov` (first fleet sets sail; `GW_FPROG=0..1` how far along), `GW_SHIPSEL=n` (pick out ships in the first fleet), `GW_POLPAGE=0..3` (Politics page), `GW_MINISTRY=n` (open that ministry), `GW_ARMYPANEL=id` (a general's panel), `GW_ASSIGN=tmpl` (the assign list), `GW_RUN=1` (the clock runs, so troops walk), `GW_MOVE=Prov` (selected divisions set off), `GW_WING=Prov` (wings on a mission there, `GW_WINGYEAR=y` their era), `GW_SHOTFRAMES=n` (shoot after n frames: compare two to check motion), `GW_LOAD=path` (start from a save), `GW_LEVELS=Prov:type:level;...` (set building levels, in `B_` order), `GW_BLUEPRINTS=/path/out.png` (with `GW_SHOT` and `GW_AUTO`: a contact sheet of one category's blueprints, nine to a sheet; `GW_BPCAT=n`, `GW_BPFROM=n`, `GW_BPEMPTY=1` for no modules; reports any chassis without its own drawing), `GW_CLICK=frame:x:y;frame:x:y` (click there on the virtual canvas, about 1400 wide, to test what a click does). `GW_MODELFROM=n` shows models from that index, larger, on the `GW_MODELS` sheet. `GW_FLAGS=/path/out.png` renders a contact sheet of every flag instead, `GW_MODELS=/path/out.png` one of every unit model. The hook is in `App::run` (`src/app/app.cpp`). Settings in `~/.local/share/greatwar1906/settings.cfg` apply to screenshots too.
- `make flags`: fetch the real flags into `data/flags/TAG.png` from Wikimedia Commons with `tools/fetch_flags.py` (`tools/flags_src.txt` says which file each nation uses; author and licence land in `data/flags/CREDITS.txt`). `nations.txt` names them as `flag = "image TAG"`; `draw_flag` loads them on first use and rebel cantons draw over them.
- `make focus`: regenerate `data/subideologies.txt` and the generated focus files (`data/focus/*_ideology.txt`, `*_currents.txt`, `generic_paths.txt`) from `tools/focus_src/*.txt` with `tools/gen_focus.py`. Edit the source, never the output.
- There is no unit-test suite. Determinism lets you check the simulation instead: the RNG is in `World::rng` and saves include it, so save → load → tick must reproduce the same state.

## Architecture

- `src/sim/` is the whole simulation and must not include raylib. `defs.h` holds the enums, name tables, `Mods`, `TerrainInfo` and templates; `world.h` declares the state structs and `World`, whose methods are spread over `load.cpp`, `geo.cpp`, `world.cpp`, `diplomacy.cpp`, `military.cpp`, `air.cpp`, `intel.cpp` (espionage), `society.cpp` (the home front), `orders.cpp` (standing division orders), `armies.cpp` (armies, generals and admirals, battle plans), `sea.cpp` (sea routes), `fleets.cpp` (fleets, and what a nation can see of foreign forces), `terrain.cpp` (the terrain table and supply), `equipment.cpp` (chassis, modules, designs, stockpile, production), `divisions.cpp` (templates, kits, division stats, experience), `weather.cpp`, `unrest.cpp` (riots, independence wars, civil wars), `script.cpp`, `ai.cpp` and `save.cpp`. `World::hour` is only the clock (the sim ticks once a day; the app advances it, from 1 hour a minute to 1 day a second). Air wings fly missions over a target province; `air_cover()` feeds battles in `military.cpp`. The AI caps its air arm with `air_cap()`. A change to dynamic state needs a matching change in `save.cpp` (and a bump of `VERSION`). One `World::tick_day()` advances everything. AI nations run `ai_daily`, plus a staggered `ai_weekly`.
- **Home front** (`society.cpp`): income → budget shares → development indices (each drifting toward a target) → class moods → stability and party drift. Wealth is measured against the great powers' average (`World::ref_wealth`, recomputed at the start of every `tick_day`), so indices don't all saturate as the world industrialises. `recompute()` order matters for determinism: `development_mods()` first (saved state only), then `compute_income()` and `compute_spirits()` last (today's food, money and moods). Anything derived that a later step reads must be computed inside `recompute`, not carried over from yesterday, or save → load → tick diverges. For the same reason `ref_wealth` reads `factories_now` (saved state), and `tick_day` refreshes `div_stats` after the fighting.
- **Terrain** (`terrain.cpp`, one `TERRAIN[]` table): defence, attacker and cavalry/armour multipliers, marching speed, supply reach, attrition, building cost, railway/fort upkeep (paid from the treasury, `Nation::upkeep`), wealth per head. Supply is a Dijkstra from the nation's cities (and friendly ports with a fleet) with terrain- and railway-weighted distance; out-of-supply penalties apply only at war. A nation's land composition gives dynamic spirits in `compute_spirits`.
- **Content end** (`CONTENT_END_YEAR` in `defs.h`, now 1920): `World::load` skips techs, focuses, events, decisions, programmes, equipment and commanders dated later, and whatever depends on them (`past_content_end`, `cut_techs`, `cut_focus`). The files stay; raise the constant to bring them back. The sim keeps running past it, and the player is told once (`content_end_seen`).
- **National content**: `data/focus/<TAG>.txt` and `data/techs/<TAG>.txt`. Every `data/focus/generic*.txt` file is shared (`generic_century.txt` holds the era branches: air power, total war, between the wars). A national focus file replaces every generic branch it also uses (matched by `branch`); techs keyed like a common tech are a national version (same place, `replaces`), others go to the National tab. `Nation::focus_vis`/`tech_vis` (static, built at load) say what each nation sees; `tech_available`, `focus_available` and the trees UI respect them, and `grant_tech` marks a common tech and its national version together so `has_tech` works with either.
- **Sub-ideologies** (`World::subs`, `Nation::sub`, saved): a current inside the ruling ideology, with modifiers added in `recompute`. Script: `set_subideology` (brings its ideology to power) and `has_subideology`. `set_ideology` clears it. Focus files named `<TAG>_*.txt` belong to that nation, so a nation can have several. `Focus::lane` and `Focus::ideo` draw the column nameplates and colours on the focus screen. Generated trees carry low AI weights (at most 1.5, against 3 to 6 in the hand-written trees), so the AI still takes the pre-1914 focuses first.
- **Ministries** (`ministries.cpp`, `data/ministries.txt`, `Nation::minister`, saved; `MIN_*` in `defs.h`): one minister per post, modifiers added in `recompute`. Names come from a hash, so appointments never touch the RNG. The first type listed in each ministry has no effects and is what everyone starts with, so the world's balance rests on it. The AI reviews its cabinet yearly, deterministically (`ai_cabinet`). A decision with `ministry = <key>` shows under that post on the Politics tab's Ministries page. Script: `has_minister`, `set_minister`.
- **Programmes** (`World::programmes`, `data/programmes/`, `Nation::prog*`, saved): repeatable campaigns in a second slot beside the focus. `programme_days` applies the `speed` factors (through `ai_weight`), `start_programme` rolls `variance`. Keep their effects one-off and small: they repeat for a century, so anything permanent compounds.
- **Campaigns** (`World::start_campaign`, `Nation::campaign`, saved, `CAMPAIGN_*` in `world.h`): the peaceful way to raise an ideology's support, player only (the AI never starts one, so balance is untouched). Paid in influence; adds to the stability target. A civil war cannot start for the ideology being campaigned for, and `set_ideology` to it costs 2 stability instead of 10 and sets `Nation::calm_until` (no civil war for a year). Buttons on the Politics tab and in the focus detail panel when a `pop_x > n` trigger is unmet.
- **Reports** (`World::completed`, not saved): `tick_research` and `tick_focus` queue what the player just finished; `tick_time` pauses, `App::draw_completed` (`trees.cpp`) shows one at a time and Continue restarts time. Effects that force a focus do not report. While this or a `Choice` is up, `App::frame` hides the mouse from everything under it.
- **Fleets** (`fleets.cpp`, `Nation::fleets`, saved): each fleet holds its own ships (`Fleet::ships`, design to count); over all fleets they add up to the stock, and `sync_fleet_ships` (daily) puts new ships in the first fleet and takes losses from the last. `Fleet::share` is derived from them, and `navy_on(n, mission)` is what the rules read. Fleets do not fight each other. Selection is `App::sel_fleets` plus `App::sel_ships` (single ships picked out; they sail off as a new fleet through `detach_ships`). Selecting fleets clears divisions and the reverse.
- **Sea routes** (`sea.cpp`): A* on a 20 km grid built from the province map on first use, with straits and canals cut by hand (`cuts()`; Panama only from 1914). Pure function of map and year, cached, never saved. `sea_route`, `sea_connected` (a Caspian port reaches no ocean port), `voyage_days`. Fleets and troopships take their time and position from the route (`SeaRoute::at`). Note `Province::coastal` is also true for lake shores, so check `sea_connected` before treating a province as a port.
- **Armies and commanders** (`armies.cpp`, `Nation::armies`, `commanders`, `Division::army`, saved; roster in `data/commanders.txt`): `refresh_commands` turns skills into `Division::cmd_atk/cmd_def/cmd_log` at the start of `tick_military`. They are derived, so nothing may read them earlier in the tick. Commanders learn from battle (`credit_commander`) and retire by year. AI great powers form armies a dozen divisions at a time (`ai_armies`), and at load every great power starts that way, player included. Names of promoted officers come from a hash, so promotions never touch the RNG.
- **Forces panel** (`src/app/forces.cpp`): always on the map at the lower right. Every army under its general, and unassigned divisions grouped by template. Clicking an army opens its general's panel above it. It shrinks to a strip when the side panel and a province panel leave no room; panels on the bottom of the map stay inside `forces_bound()` and above `forces_reserve()`. `draw_forces_popups` draws its open lists last, and `App::frame` hides the mouse from the rest while one is open. Sim helpers: `assign_divisions`, `dissolve_army`.
- **Battle plans** (`Army::plan`, `tick_plans`, drawn by `map_plans.cpp`, input in `App::input_map`): front line (a nation's whole border; the line itself is drawn in the globe shader from palette row 4, so it lies exactly on the border at every zoom, magenta for plans and dark red for plain war fronts; `map_plans.cpp` adds the teeth and label), defence line (painted provinces), attack (waypoints; readiness grows to +20% attack while preparing and burns off in action). Divisions spread by length of border (`front_need`, `spread_to`; also Hold the front). Followed by divisions with `ORD_PLAN`; a plain move takes a division out of the plan until the plan is given again. Player only: the AI has armies but no plans.
- **Fog** (`World::forces_view`, `ForcesView::sees`, rebuilt each frame as `MapView::fog`): foreign divisions, fleets and air wings show only with `intel_on >= INTEL_MIL` (allies count as 60), or at war when in contact with our land or troops. Display only: the sim and the AI never read it. New UI that shows foreign forces must check it.
- **Weather on the map** (`src/view/map_weather.cpp`, display only): a cloud shell above the globe, from a texture of `World::sky_at` (`texture6` of the globe too: today's and tomorrow's cloud, blended by the clock; snow lying; wet ground). Rain, snow, sand, fog and lightning close up belong to spots on the ground and read the weather there. `cfg.weather` turns the visuals off. The climate (`climate` in `weather.cpp`) comes from latitude and `inland_km`, both pure functions of the map.
- **Coastal forts and airfields** (`B_COAST`, `B_AIRFIELD`, `B_AIRBASE`, `B_AIRSHIP`, `B_SEAPLANE`, `B_AIRPORT`; `Province::level(type)`, saved; `BUILD_ICON`, `BUILD_SHORT`, `BUILD_ABOUT` in `tab_industry.cpp`): a wing can only be based at a matching airfield with room (`can_base`, `airfield_for`, 2 wings a level); a capital takes anything without limit, so nobody loses the air. Nobody starts with a base: the tech that opens a kind brings the first one to the capital (`tech_base`, from `grant_tech`). `ocean_coast` excludes lakes and the Caspian. Coastal forts disorder and weaken landings and lessen blockades. `MapView::field_spot` says where each stands.
- **Animation** runs on `MapView::anim_t`, which only advances while `App::time_running()`, so a paused game is a still picture (the front line's pulse and counters gliding to a new spot stay on real time). It never touches the sim. Armies, aircraft and ships in `map_sky.cpp`: soldiers and riders have swinging legs (`MD_SOLDIER_BODY`, `MD_LEG`, `MD_RIDER_BODY`, `MD_HORSE_LEG`); an aircraft's position is a function of time (`plane_at`: out from its base, orbit, home) and heading and bank come from it; fleets, troopships and marching counters use the clock between the daily ticks (`World::hour`). `MapView::running` stops the marching while paused. The Solar System takes orbits and spin from the game date at real periods, so it follows the game's speed and pause.
- **Build tool**: Industry tab sets `App::build_tool`; `input_map` then takes over the mouse (click, shift fills, right-click calls `World::unbuild`), `MapView::build_mark` draws the dots.
- **Aide-de-camp** (`src/app/advisor.cpp`): `advisor_scan` turns the state of the nation into prioritised tips and never changes the sim; only `advisor_act` does, on the player's click. It is UI state, not saved. New warnings go in `advisor_scan`. Treasury tips read the recurring flow (reserve less upkeep and interest), never the change in the balance, so one-off spending doesn't look like a deficit.
- **Equipment** (`equipment.h`): chassis + modules (data/equipment/) unlocked by techs become per-nation `Design`s (global `World::designs`, saved; `design_stats` derives stats and `score`). Stock is `Nation::stock` (design → pieces). Lines build designs. Templates (`DivTemplate`, battalions from data/battalions.txt) aggregate `Need`s (category, role, weight); a `Division::kit` holds (design, count) per need and `div_stats` turns it into `Division::st` (derived, recomputed daily and after load). Combat uses soft/hard attack against hardness, armour against piercing, width-based frontage. AI: `refresh_designs` on research, `ai_production`, `ai_recruit` from standard templates by kind.
- **Weather** is a pure function of the day (`compute_weather_all` at the start of `tick_day` and after load); never touches the RNG. **Unrest** is per province (saved). Civil wars use twelve dormant `rebel_slot` nations appended at load (`RB01`..), saved with their name, flag and colour.
- **This world's war** (`data/events/alt_crisis.txt`, WORLD.md): the great war comes around 1910 from a French or Russian civil war or from the Upper Nile crisis. `start_civil_war` sets `rebel_of_<TAG>` on the rebels and the global flag `civil_war_<TAG>`, so events can answer: the rebels join the UK's faction, the UK uses `join_faction_wars` (every war of its faction members, with its subjects), and the other Holy Alliance crown `join_ally_wars`. A nation with the `neutral` flag (`flags = { neutral }` in nations.txt; Germany) cannot declare war, join or found factions, or be called in by guarantees and ententes; its Weltpolitik focus clears it.
- **Colonial regimes** (`Province::regime`, saved; `regime=model|plunder` in `tools/cities_1906.txt`, carried by mapgen): plunder colonies yield 35% more and seethe, model colonies yield 10% less and settle (`tick_unrest`, `compute_income`). Script: `reform_colonies`, `model_colonies_policy`, `squeeze_colonies`, triggers `plunder_colonies`, `model_colonies`.
- **Food**: grain and livestock. Need is the urban share of the population (industry + middle class), not all of it: peasants feed themselves. `Nation::diet` is the habitual grain share; it drifts toward what is actually eaten over about 20 years.
- **Map pipeline**:
  1. Offline, `tools/mapgen.cpp` rasterises Natural Earth admin-1 units and the 1906 frontiers (`tools/world.poly`) onto the GW×GH = 8192×4096 grid (about 5 km cells). It cuts units along the frontiers, merges small pieces and splits big multi-city ones. Each piece inherits owner, status and `release=` from a city in `tools/cities_1906.txt` (a city in the same admin-1 unit and 1906 country first, else the nearest; so to move a border along a state line, give that state its own city), and terrain comes from `data/elevation.png` + `tools/_dl/earth.jpg` (Blue Marble, made by the Makefile). Towns from `tools/towns_1906.txt` split any piece over 16,000 km² that holds one; a town piece counts as countryside (owner, people and food from its nearest city), with a `town` flag for a few houses on the map. It writes `provinces.txt` (3,733 provinces: about 580 with a real city, the rest size-0 countryside with no victory points) and the run-length-encoded `provinces.map`.
  2. At startup `World::load_map` (`geo.cpp`) reads the map into `World::pmap`. Adjacency and coast flags come from touching cells, plus `link;A;B` lines. `alias=` names (cities merged into a bigger province) also resolve through `prov_of`, so events can still name them.
  3. The sim never sees elevation or imagery: terrain is the `ter=` flag.
  4. `load_map` fills holes of up to 5 cells ringed by land (pin-pricks mapgen leaves, not lakes) with the province that touches them most, so they neither count as coast nor show as blots on a map that inks every shore. A city's or town's province carries `region=<admin-1 unit>` (`Province::region`, static): the Godot game shows it as the province's name with the city as its chief town; `prov_of` and events still use the city name.
- **Scale**: world units are kilometres (`GLOBE_R` 6371); `MapView::dist` and the zoom thresholds stay in degrees of arc (`KM_PER_UNIT` converts). `MapView::begin3d` replaces BeginMode3D to set near/far planes from the camera height. A sixth texture (`texture5`, 8192×4096, built at startup in `mapview.cpp`) holds the built-up land: fractal city patches, roads between cities, scattered farms. The shader lights it after dark (glow and a mip-blurred halo far off, `streets()` close up, brightness from palette row 3) and paints roofs and streets from it by day. Palette row 4 marks the provinces on a front.
- **Rendering** (`src/view/`: `mapview.cpp` setup/labels/camera, `map_shader.cpp` GLSL, `map_sky.cpp` 3D (aircraft, ground units, cities, fleets, satellites), `map_models.cpp` the unit and building meshes (`ship_model_for` picks a warship's by role, weight and date), `map_plans.cpp` battle plans, front teeth and routes, `map_weather.cpp` clouds and precipitation, `map_overlay.cpp` 2D, `map_solar.cpp` the Solar System past the farthest zoom, with planets on their real orbits and exploration from `techs/common_space.txt`): the map is a globe of radius `GLOBE_R` (1 unit ≈ 1° ≈ 111 km), made of 128 indexed mesh tiles (2048×1024 quads). Heights in `vheight` come from `data/elevation.png` at 5× true height. The shader paints a hand-drawn map from five textures:
  - a static province-id texture (grey+alpha, nearest filtering; each pixel's province is chosen by a gaussian vote over the nearest cells, looked up through a small noise warp so borders wobble like ink instead of stepping);
  - a blurred land mask for smooth coasts;
  - a tiny per-province palette (fill colour with tint strength in alpha, owner id with selected/hover flags, occupier colour, night lights, fronts). It's rewritten every frame in `rebuild_texture`, so map changes are instant. `PAL_W` (4096) caps the province count;
  - `data/terrain.jpg`, the painted Terrain-mode image. raylib is built without JPEG support, so `stb_image` (static) decodes it in `mapview.cpp`;
  - an aux texture built at startup (grey+alpha, so the shader reads `.r` and `.a`): rivers rasterised from `data/geo.txt` as a soft tent, and the chamfer distance out to sea (sea shading and contour lines).

  Land and sea come only from the province map, never from imagery, so coasts and borders always match. Political modes are parchment plus watercolour washes; the national-border band is darkened by sampling owners a few cells away. Hill shading uses mesh normals; `lightDir` is the sun (`sun_direction` in `map_internal.h`, noon at the player's capital at 12:00) or a fixed light when day-night is off. Closer than `RIVER_LINES` the overlay draws rivers as vector lines and the shader fades its raster rivers.

  Borders are found in the shader by comparing ids a pixel away on screen. Nation label places (`place_labels`: largest piece of the homeland, distance transform, best row run for the name's aspect) recompute only when `World::map_dirty` is set; their size is fitted each frame and they fade out close up. Smaller names use an occupancy list so they never overlap. The camera looks straight down at (`lat`, `lon`) from `dist` above the surface (0.35 to 175), and below `TILT_FROM` leans back from the south, up to `TILT_MAX`, so models show their sides. Division counters and labels are 2D overlays drawn in real pixels by `draw_overlay`. Divisions in a province on a front stand along its border with the enemy (`MapView::front_spots`, display only: to the sim they are in the province) and glide between spots. Unit models (`map_models.cpp`: lit meshes built at startup, nation colour marked in the vertex alpha, drawn with `ModelSet::draw` plus a flattened `shadow`) have one fixed size in km at every zoom (`SHIP_KM`, `MAN_KM` and the rest in `map_sky.cpp`, exaggerated because true size is under a pixel) and stop drawing past `SHIPS_VISIBLE`, `AIRCRAFT_VISIBLE` and `GROUND_VISIBLE`. Ground models and city buildings stand straight up, each on the highest point under its footprint (`ground_under`); `MapView::ground` follows the mesh's own triangles. Fleets always draw, at most `SQUADRON_MAX` ships each (every class, then more); planes only when a wing is on a mission. Smoke and balloons still scale with `k = dist / 20`. Cities are laid out once per province (`layouts` in `map_sky.cpp`, a turned grid sorted from the centre out, deterministic from hashes, never saved): tenements and town hall in the core, terraces, then houses; only the core draws past `dist` 3.2. Vertex alpha about a half marks a window (`windows()` in `map_models.cpp`): dark by day, lit by night, with the daylight passed in the draw colour's alpha.
- **Equipment blueprint**: `tab_designer.cpp` keeps the sheet (`blueprint_ground`), slot boxes, parts schedule and title block; `blueprint.cpp` draws the machine with `blueprint_render` (side, plan and front). Every chassis has a row in its category's table (`VEHS`, `SHIPS`, `AIRS`, `GUNS`, `RIFLES`, `TRUCKS`, `LOCOS`, `TRACTORS`), keyed by chassis id, in real metres, with its mass and crew. Each row is modelled on one real type. A `SHIPS` row is the whole arrangement of its class (broken deck line, deckhouses, funnels, masts, mounts). Aircraft and armour also have a shape table, `AIRSHAPES` (fuselage stations, wings, fin, pods, canopy) and `VEHSHAPES` (hull and turret outlines; turrets stand on the hull with `top_at`). To add a chassis, add a row (and a shape row for aircraft and armour); one without a row borrows the last in the table and shows up on `GW_BLUEPRINTS` as NO DRAWING. Drawing happens twice with the same code: first the `Pen` only measures (`measuring`), then it draws, so length, height and scale are read from the drawing and a longer gun really lengthens it. Parts are filled with the paper colour, so draw back to front. What is fitted changes the drawing (`Fit`, read from module names). A draw function calls `p.mark(slot, x, y)` for each part: the leader from that slot's box ends there, so call it for every slot, fitted or empty. `p.kit` marks what stands beside the machine (a limber, a wagon, an aerial) and is left out of its dimensions. These figures are not rules: the sim reads only `design_stats`. **Map symbols**: `draw_unit_symbol` and `UNIT_SYMBOLS` in `ui/icons.cpp`, shared by templates and counters. A list opened in a panel must ignore the click that opened it (`list_was_open`).
- **UI themes** (`UI::set_theme`, `theme_info`, `enum Theme` in `ui.h`): `pal::` is mutable and `set_theme` rewrites it, so everything drawn afterwards takes the category's colours. `UI::pattern` draws the faint per-theme ground, `UI::frame` the corners and `UI::banner` the panel's title strip. `UI::begin` and `draw_tab` reset to `TH_DEFAULT`. `pal::GOOD`, `BAD`, `WARN` and `SKY` stay constant.
- **Picking** (`MapView::ground_hit`, `painted_province`, `mapview.cpp`): a click marches the ray through the drawn relief, built from `cam` as `begin3d` projects (raylib's unprojection is too coarse 6,400 km from the origin), then answers with the shader's own lookup: the warp (`warp_noise`, an integer hash written identically in C++ and GLSL), the vote of the nearest cells, and the smoothed coast (`land_mask`). Change one and change the other.
- **Projecting to the screen**: a point must pass `MapView::facing` before `GetWorldToScreen`. Close to the ground most of the near side is over the horizon or behind the camera, and a plain dot product with the camera direction lets it through, which mirrors it onto the screen.
- **Settings** (`Settings` in `app.h`, `settings.cpp`): read before `InitWindow`, because anti-aliasing and vsync are window flags. `apply_frame_limit` runs every frame (15 fps out of focus). Presets are the table `PRESETS`; "custom" is simply no preset matching. raylib's `ToggleBorderlessWindowed` also pins the window on top, so `apply_settings` clears `FLAG_WINDOW_TOPMOST`.
- **Fonts** (`assets/fonts`, `UI::load_fonts`): Fira Sans Condensed Medium and SemiBold, Playfair Display SC for titles. Mipmapped, because the atlas is 64 px and most text about 13.
- **Division index** (`World::divs_at`, `div_index`, `military.cpp`): which divisions stand in each province, so `hostile_divs_in` and `divs_in` need no scan of all divisions. Anything that changes a division's `prov`, `alive` or `sea_days` must call `unindex_div` before and `index_div` after, or set `div_index_ok = false` (also for adding or erasing divisions); a stale index silently changes the war. To check it, compare the lists against a full scan at every query over several seeds.
- **Projection**: use `MapView::to_screen`, not `GetWorldToScreen`, on the map: raylib rebuilds both camera matrices on every call.
- **Checking sim changes**: `--simulate DAYS SEED` is deterministic, so a change meant to be pure optimisation must give identical output (ignore the line with the elapsed time) over several seeds.
- **UI scale**: `UI::begin` picks a scale so the virtual canvas is about 1400×760 (times `user_scale`, which the pause menu adjusts), and `ui.push()`/`ui.pop()` wrap all interface drawing. Inside the UI, use `ui.W()`/`ui.H()`, `ui.scissor()` and `ui.mouse_delta()`, never the raw screen size or mouse. Cut text to width with `ui.fit` (never `substr`: names hold UTF-8). A character outside `codepoints()` in `ui.cpp` draws as `?`. Map code works in real pixels.
- **Scripting**: every content file uses one block-format parser (`src/script/parser.*`). Triggers are `World::trig_one` and effects are `World::eff_one` in `script.cpp`, each with a matching `describe_*` used for tooltips. A new trigger or effect needs a branch there plus a description, or it shows up as a load-time "unknown effect" warning. Event and focus `Node*` pointers point into `World::docs`, so those documents must stay alive.
- **Player choices** (events, calls to arms, offers) are `Choice` objects in `World::choices` that hold lambdas capturing `this`. The UI pops them; the AI resolves its own events in `fire_event` using the `ai` weights.
- **Focus and research screens** (`src/app/trees.cpp`): category tabs filter the tree. Focus tabs are the `branch` values the nation sees, its own branches first; `branch_title` and `branch_blurb` give their names and one-line explanations. Research tabs come from `tech_groups()` (empty groups are hidden), so a new tech `cat` must be added there; otherwise it lands in the last tab (National). `focus_cat_of` groups the focus branches under headings; an unlisted branch lands under National. The research layout is computed, not taken from the data: a tech's row comes from its `year` and its column from its parents, so `x`/`y` in tech files only break ties, and a tech must never require a later-dated one. The focus tree does use the data's `x`/`y`, with cells sized to fill the panel. Every branch runs general to specific: one root, then two to four general directions, then specific chains under each; ideology trees are the root, four pillars (Monarchy, Democracy, Socialism, Nationalism) and two or three country currents per pillar. Focuses never wait on the calendar: a dated focus uses `era > Y`, true once the nation's research reaches Y (`World::tech_era`, its fifth-newest tech), so later currents and people stay out of the 1910s without timers. `gen_focus.py` writes `era` gates only for currents dated after 1912, and their steps follow by prerequisite alone. The ideology sources (`tools/focus_src`) keep to ideas of 1906-1918 in this world: one current per ideology for a great power, two for a minor; there are no modern currents, Cold War, space or information-age branches. Focus pictures: `data/icons/focus/<key>.png` (white on transparent) replaces a focus's vector icon when the file exists (`focus_ic`, `focus_pic` in trees.cpp); a current's steps share the current's picture and the four ideology pillars share `pillar_monarchy`, `pillar_democracy`, `pillar_socialism`, `pillar_nationalism`. `tools/import_icons.sh FOLDER` turns images drawn white on black into those PNGs. Detail panels are built as a `Doc` and drawn with `doc_draw`, which shrinks the text to fit and scrolls only if that is not enough.
- **App files** (`src/app/`): `app.cpp` loop/input/time, `hud.cpp` top bar and overlays, `screens.cpp` title/pick/pause, `settings.cpp` settings and autosave, `tab_designer.cpp` (the Production screen's equipment blueprint and division grid: an open list takes the mouse from everything else on the page), `province_panel.cpp`, `country.cpp`, `trees.cpp`, `society.cpp`, and one `tab_*.cpp` per side panel (`panels.cpp` only dispatches).
- **UI** (`src/ui/ui.*`): immediate mode. Map input is skipped when the previous frame's panels covered the mouse (`ui.over_prev`). raylib defines `GOLD`, `BLUE` and similar colour names as macros, so the UI palette (`pal::`) avoids them.

## Balance notes (from tuning with `--simulate` over several seeds)

The intended feel is a pre-war build-up, then a WWI-style stalemate that ends in negotiated peace around 1917–18, with history diverging between runs. These levers keep it that way:
- trench digging (`Division::dig`), combat frontage, and a square-rooted force ratio in `military.cpp`;
- the 60-day capitulation timer in `check_capitulation`;
- war exhaustion (`Nation::war_days`);
- relation drift toward `World::base_rel`;
- `improve_relations` costs 15 influence plus 5 for every approach made to that nation lately (`Nation::courting`, saved; one approach is forgotten a year), and its +8 arrives over 60 days (`tick_courting`), for the AI as for the player;
- low-weight focuses only being picked rarely (`ai_weekly`);
- AI reserves sent to front provinces in proportion to the enemy strength facing them (`threat` in `ai_military`). With thousands of small provinces, nearest-empty-first deployment let whole armies drain into one theatre and both sides blitzed.
- factory cost rising past one factory per million core people (`build_cost_at`). The AI keeps four builds queued, so without it industry compounded at about 15% a year on the old 2,001-province map;
- terrain supply reach (800 km of plains-equivalent, railways and terrain stretch it) and attrition; railway upkeep coefficients in `compute_income` (about a third of what the reserve saves for France);
- `Province::rail_weight`: countryside counts 0.45 for railway upkeep and locomotive need, so splitting provinces with towns doesn't make railways cost more;
- class moods count only `0.2 ×` toward stability, and development effects are centred on 60 (what a typical budget buys). Larger values pushed every rich nation to 100 stability and let broken Russia recover in a few years.

When AI behaviour looks wrong, trace it with a scratch program linked against `src/sim/*.cpp`; don't guess.

- **Architecture questions**: `get_architecture_overview_tool` + `list_communities_tool`

- Narrow scope with the graph, then read the source. Do not change code from graph output alone.

- Verify the exact source when touching behavior, database logic, migrations, retries, fallbacks,
  recovery, or compatibility code.
- When the graph and the source disagree, the source wins. The graph may be stale or may not
  model that relationship.
- An empty graph result can mean "not indexed" or "not statically visible", not "does not exist".
